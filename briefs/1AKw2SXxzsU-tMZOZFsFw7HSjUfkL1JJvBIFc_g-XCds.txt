Clipping Campaign Brief for Sandisk Combined Creators Clipping
Campaign Information
1. Client Name: Cheat Code Agency
2. Campaign Display Name: Sandisk Stream Clipping
3. Campaign Objective: Distribute clips and edits across socials from the Sandisk sponsored TenZ, Doublelift, FNS and NiceWigg streams to drive awareness of Sandisk and spend down the remaining campaign budget before close.
4. Clipper Budget: $5,000
5. CPM: $2.00 per 1,000 views
6. Country Demographics: Tier 1 and Tier 2 (US focus, English-speaking audiences)
7. Platforms Eligible: TikTok - IG Reels - YT Shorts
8. Campaign Launch Date: Immediate, launching as soon as approved
Campaign Requirements:
* Clipper Guidelines + Requirements:
   * Must be a clip or edit from the provided content in the clip bank (the four links in clip bank)
   * Must mention “Sandisk” somewhere in the post caption. Always spell it Sandisk, lowercase d, never SanDisk.
   * Must include #SandiskCollaborator in the caption.

Key Information: (summary of campaign for context)
Sandisk has run a series of sponsored gaming streams pairing top pro players against a masked opponent in a red helmet, referred to on screen as OP. This flight combines the remaining budget across all four streams into one final push, TenZ (Valorant), Doublelift (League of Legends), FNS (Valorant) and NiceWigg (Apex Legends). Each stream is built around Sandisk branding, with lower third overlays, a countdown to chat giveaways, and Sandisk product callouts running throughout. The content bank is four stringouts, so the best moments have already been pulled and clippers should work from those, pulling from whichever stringout they want. The goal is awareness for Sandisk with a gaming audience, so every clip needs to keep Sandisk in the caption and visible on screen while still feeling like a highlight a fan would post. Angles to lean into: big plays and clutch moments from TenZ, Doublelift, FNS and NiceWigg, four of the most recognizable names in their games; the masked OP opponent, where the reveal, the reactions, and the mystery of who is behind the helmet are the hook, kept as a person and never an AI or a robot; player reactions and commentary that cut well at 10 to 20 seconds; the chat giveaway countdown moments that give viewers a reason to look up the full stream; and side by side edits of the pro versus OP, which suit the split screen format. Clips should feel like a gaming highlight page found the best 15 seconds of the stream, with Sandisk naturally in frame, not like an ad.
